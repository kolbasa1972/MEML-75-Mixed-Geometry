==========================================================================
  §10 Effective alpha and the b_u/c_u problem
==========================================================================

  alpha_ab = ln(b_u/a_u) / (d13^2 - d12^2) = 13.21
  [diagnostic only] no explicit manuscript target for alpha_ab;
  the 'alpha ~ 10, spread 1.7' phrase was in a removed appendix.

  b_u / c_u = 184.66  (i.e., c_u is 0.0054 of b_u)
  Simple exp(-alpha d^2) with O(1) prefactor would give:
    b_u/c_u = exp(-alpha * (d_x - d12^2))   for some distance d_x
  With alpha_ab = 13.21 (computed above) and b_u/c_u = 184.7, we need
    d_x - d12^2 = ln(185)/13.21 = 0.395
  i.e. d_x^2 = 0.693.
  Available distances: d12^2 = 0.298, d13^2 = 1.192.
  NEITHER matches -> c_u needs a non-O(1) prefactor A_c,
  or is at a position not captured by the simple 3-point picture.
  [NOTE] This is a genuine limitation of the qualitative
         interpretation in §10. It does not affect the
         quantitative fit, but should be stated explicitly.
  (v24.5.2: using the computed alpha_ab instead of the old
   hardcoded alpha=10 leaves the conclusion unchanged: with
   alpha=10 one would need d_x^2 ≈ 0.82;
   with alpha_ab=13.21 one needs d_x^2 ≈ 0.69. Both match
   neither d12^2=0.30 nor d13^2=1.19.)
