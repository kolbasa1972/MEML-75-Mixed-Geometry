 Y_101: alpha=+1.00, |Y|=1.004088, |Y_000/Y_ijk|=1.0000

 Result: for M=2 setup, all allowed Y_ijk have integer alpha
 -> |Y_ijk| = |Y_000| -> ratio = 1 (not 65).
 The paper's 65 does not come from this simple setup.

--- Systematic scan: which (M_Q, M_u, M_H) gives ~65? ---
 Closest to 65: M_Q=6, M_u=-2, M_H=-4
 |Y_000/Y_012| = 1.0000 (target 65)
 Still cannot give 65 with small fluxes in 1-modulus setup

 Interpretation: 1-modulus CI+M Yukawa on magnetized T^2
 gives ratios ~1 for integer alpha, and ratios 0.5-2 for
 fractional alpha. Getting 65 requires either:
  (a) a specific cross-modulus product, or
  (b) Kähler metric factors that differ between zero modes, or
  (c) a multi-modulus T^2/Z_3 × T^2/Z_4 setup (mixed orbifold).
 None of these are specified in the paper; §13.4 must specify
 the exact flux assignment to make the 65 claim reproducible.

 PART 3: SO(10) for y_nu
==============================================================================

 y_nu (fit at M_6) = 0.934
 y_t(M_GUT) = 0.483
 SO(10) at M_GUT: y_nu = 2 y_t = 0.9660

 Direct comparison at M_GUT:
   y_nu_fit / y_nu_SO10 = 0.9669
   Deviation = 3.31%
   Paper claim: ~3% agreement -> CONSISTENT at M_GUT

 M_6/M_GUT = 1.480
 Delta log = 0.3920
 One-loop MSSM running (3 y_t^2 term only):
   y_nu(M_6)/y_nu(M_GUT) = 1 + 0.0017
   = 1.0017
   -> negligible running

 Old v24.4 claim: y_nu^fit / y_nu^RGE = 1.95
 Implied: y_nu^RGE = 0.4790
 Compare: y_nu_SO10(M_GUT) = 0.9660
 y_nu_SO10 / y_nu_RGE = 2.0168
 RGE from M_GUT to M_6 alone reduces y_nu by factor ~1.0017
 -> RGE from M_GUT to M_6 alone CANNOT explain the ~1.38/2.28 gap.

 Naive long-interval RGE (GUT to M_SUSY ~ 1 TeV):
 Delta log = 30.63
 Factor y_nu(TeV)/y_nu(GUT) = exp(3 y_t^2 log / 16pi^2) = 1.3299
 This IS O(2)! But it's running GUT -> TeV, not GUT -> M_6.

 --- Small tanβ scenario: y_t(M_GUT) = 0.7 ---
 SO(10) at M_GUT: y_nu = 2 y_t = 1.4000
 (cf. paper: y_t(M_GUT) ~ 0.7 for small tanβ)
 Long-interval RGE factor (GUT->TeV, y_t_avg=0.85) = 1.5225
 y_nu(M_SUSY) from minimal SO(10) = 2.1316
 Fit needs y_nu = 0.934
 Ratio (requires 126_H) = 2.28
 Paper claim for small tanβ: factor ~2.28

 Sensitivity: RGE factor vs assumed y_t_avg (small tanβ):
   y_t_avg=0.70 -> RGE factor 1.330, ratio to fit 1.99
   y_t_avg=0.75 -> RGE factor 1.387, ratio to fit 2.08
   y_t_avg=0.80 -> RGE factor 1.451, ratio to fit 2.18
   y_t_avg=0.85 -> RGE factor 1.523, ratio to fit 2.28
   y_t_avg=0.90 -> RGE factor 1.602, ratio to fit 2.40
   y_t_avg=0.95 -> RGE factor 1.691, ratio to fit 2.53
   y_t_avg=1.00 -> RGE factor 1.789, ratio to fit 2.68

 Interpretation: the paper's large-tanβ factor (~1.38) and
 small-tanβ factor (~2.28) both come from running between
 GUT scale and SUSY scale (long interval), NOT from M_GUT to M_6
 (short interval, factor ~1.0017).
 With long-interval running, y_nu at M_SUSY is factor ~1.3-1.5 larger
 than at M_GUT, which is compatible with the claim.
 Verdict: minimal SO(10) (y_nu = 2 y_t at GUT) gives y_nu(M_SUSY)
 = 1.285 (large tanβ), but fit needs 0.934. Factor discrepancy
 1.38 requires 126_H.
